Digital sustainability
is environmental sustainability

Permacomputing applies permaculture principles to digital technology—working with nature rather than against it.

Every byte of data we generate consumes energy and resources. By designing systems that require less, we reduce our digital footprint on the planet.

From solar-powered servers to software that works on decade-old hardware, we're cultivating a more resilient digital ecosystem.

Permacomputing Principles

A holistic approach to computing that respects planetary boundaries.

Appropriate Technology

Choose the simplest technology that solves the problem effectively. Not every solution needs cloud computing or AI.

Regenerative Design

Create systems that get better over time, not worse. Software should improve with use rather than becoming obsolete.

Longevity

Build for the long term. Design hardware and software that can be maintained and repaired for decades, not just years.

Fair Resource Use

Distribute computational resources justly and equitably. Consider the global impacts of your digital practices.

Simplicity

Embrace minimal, elegant solutions. Complexity increases fragility and resource consumption.

Cyclical Thinking

Consider the full lifecycle of technology. Design for reuse, repair, and eventual biodegradation or recycling.
